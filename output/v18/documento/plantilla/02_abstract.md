## Abstract

For each of nine factors, the paper reproduces and replicates published estimates for Spain, extends them to more periods and territories where the data allow, and reports a preferred magnitude, an interval and a V18 classification. All V18 figures are exploratory (project layer C4). The design is an internal pre-registration without an external timestamp (git tag plan-replicacion-v18, commit ad4385c, 2026-10-20); the V17 audit, the robustness checks added after the first results and the deviations are declared as post hoc.

Results are mostly negative. No own estimate reaches the IDENTIFIED class. One is SUGGESTIVE (rent caps in Catalonia, E2-R1); the others are ASSOCIATION, INCONCLUSIVE or NOT ESTIMABLE for lack of variation or data. Only E1, E4 and E6 have a preferred magnitude, and it comes from one or two published studies. For E2, E3 and E5 the published estimates are too heterogeneous to combine; for E7, E8 and E9 the literature gives ranges without standard errors. For rent caps, the range across studies is {{V18-B5-E2:min_num}} to {{V18-B5-E2:max_num}} in log points of rent, with no preferred magnitude.

The accounting of the price rise follows a rule fixed beforehand that admits only factors with a valid own estimate. No factor qualifies, so the whole observed change is left unexplained: in 1995Q1-2007Q4 the unexplained change in the real purchase price is {{V18-C5-1995T1-2007T4-00-NOEXPL-P:num}} percentage points (100 times the log change). An illustration with magnitudes from the literature is shown separately and is not an attribution.

Limitation sentence for the E2-R1 figure. Between 2020Q4 and 2022Q1, the average rent of new contracts registered at Incasòl in the municipalities subject to Law 11/2020 (excluding Barcelona city; sample: {{V18-E2-R1:cobertura}}) stood, relative to 2019, below that of unregulated Catalan municipalities with a tight market (log {{V18-E2-R1:sig}}; 95% CI {{V18-E2-R1:min_num}} to {{V18-E2-R1:max_num}}, rounded to two decimals). The difference is not stable: it is largest in 2021Q1 ([FALTA CIFRA: coeficiente de 2021T1 del estudio de eventos de E2-R1]), narrows gradually until it is indistinguishable from zero in 2022Q1, before Constitutional Court ruling 37/2022, with no change afterwards, and falls to [FALTA CIFRA: coeficiente de E2-R1 con trayectorias trimestrales por tamaño del municipio e IC95] when quarterly trajectories are allowed to differ by municipality size. These data cannot separate the part associated with the law from a transitory COVID-19 shock in large municipalities or from a change in the composition of registered contracts, and sensitivity to differential trends (Honest DiD with M̄ of 0.5 or more) cannot be assessed with the available power. The magnitude is consistent with Jofre-Monseny et al. (2023), who use the same register and the same episode. V18 class: SUGGESTIVE; project layer C4.

Keywords: housing prices, rents, Spain, replication, meta-analysis, exploratory evidence.
